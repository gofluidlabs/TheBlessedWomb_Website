## Introduction

Almost every woman experiences an irregular period at some point — but when irregularity becomes a pattern, it's worth understanding why. Menstrual cycles are sensitive to a wide range of influences, from hormones to stress to underlying medical conditions. This guide walks through the common causes and helps you know when it's time for a proper evaluation rather than waiting things out.

## Key Takeaways

- A "normal" cycle can range from about 21 to 35 days — irregularity means a pattern that consistently falls outside this, or unpredictable timing.
- Common causes include PMOS/PCOS, thyroid disorders, pregnancy, stress, significant weight changes, and perimenopause.
- Some medications and other gynaecological conditions can also affect cycle regularity.
- Self-medicating to induce periods isn't appropriate without first understanding the underlying cause.
- Persistent irregularity, especially with other symptoms, warrants a gynaecological evaluation.

## What Counts as Menstrual Irregularity?

Cycles vary from person to person, but general patterns that suggest irregularity include:

- Cycles consistently shorter than 21 days or longer than 35 days
- Unpredictable timing that varies significantly month to month
- Periods that are unusually heavy, prolonged, or associated with large clots
- Absence of periods for three months or more (when not pregnant)
- Bleeding between periods

A single irregular cycle is common and often not significant on its own — the pattern over time matters more than any one occurrence.

## Common Causes

### PMOS/PCOS

Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome (PMOS) — the updated name for Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) — is one of the most common causes of irregular ovulation and periods. We cover this in detail in our [PMOS/PCOS guide](/blog/polyendocrine-metabolic-ovarian-syndrome-pmos-pcos).

### Thyroid Disorders

Both an underactive and overactive thyroid can disrupt the hormonal balance that regulates menstruation, which is why thyroid function is often checked as part of an irregular-periods evaluation.

### Pregnancy

A missed or unusually light period can be an early sign of pregnancy and is generally one of the first things ruled out during evaluation.

### Stress

Significant physical or psychological stress can affect the hypothalamic signals that drive ovulation, sometimes delaying or skipping periods entirely.

### Weight Changes

Both significant weight loss and weight gain, especially when rapid, can affect hormone levels enough to disrupt cycle regularity.

### Perimenopause

As women approach menopause, hormonal shifts commonly cause cycles to become irregular before periods stop altogether. This is a normal transition, though it's still worth discussing with your gynaecologist to distinguish it from other causes.

### Medication-Related Causes

Certain medications, including some hormonal contraceptives and other prescription drugs, can influence cycle patterns. If you've recently started or stopped a medication and noticed changes, mention this to your doctor.

### Other Gynaecological Causes

Conditions such as fibroids or endometrial issues can also cause irregular or heavy bleeding, and are part of what a gynaecologist evaluates for.

## Why Evaluation Matters

Because so many different things can cause irregular periods, treating the irregularity itself — for example, by trying to induce a period without medical guidance — can mask an underlying issue that actually needs attention. Proper evaluation helps identify the actual cause, which then guides the right approach.

## What a Doctor May Ask, and Possible Tests

A typical evaluation includes:

- A detailed menstrual history — cycle length, flow, associated symptoms, and how long the irregularity has been happening
- General health history, including recent weight changes, stress, and medications
- A clinical examination
- Blood tests, often including thyroid function and other relevant hormone levels
- Pelvic ultrasound, when appropriate, to assess the uterus and ovaries

Not every test applies to every patient — your doctor will tailor the evaluation to your specific history and symptoms.

## When to See a Gynaecologist

Book an evaluation if:

- Irregularity has persisted for several cycles
- You're experiencing very heavy bleeding, severe pain, or bleeding between periods
- Your periods have stopped for three months or more and you're not pregnant
- You're trying to conceive and cycle irregularity is a concern
- Irregular periods are accompanied by other symptoms like acne, hair growth changes, or unexplained weight changes
