## Introduction

"Antenatal care" covers the whole span of checkups and monitoring through pregnancy — not just a single visit type. This guide walks through what that typically includes, end to end. If you're specifically looking for what happens in the earliest weeks, see our [first trimester guide](/blog/first-trimester-pregnancy-care); this article picks up the broader picture and complements our [patient journey page](/process), which describes the overall path of care at The Blessed Womb.

## Key Takeaways

- Antenatal care spans the first consultation through to postpartum planning, not just isolated appointments.
- Current guidance favours **tailoring visit frequency to your individual pregnancy** rather than one fixed schedule for everyone.
- Routine monitoring covers blood pressure, growth, wellbeing, and screening, adjusted to your trimester and risk profile.
- Nutrition, supplementation and vaccination decisions are individualised — discuss these with your doctor rather than following generic advice.
- Birth and postpartum planning are part of antenatal care, not an afterthought.

## 1. First Consultation

Your antenatal journey begins with a detailed consultation covering your medical history, previous pregnancies, and current health, as detailed in our [first trimester guide](/blog/first-trimester-pregnancy-care).

## 2. Pregnancy Dating and Assessment

Establishing an accurate gestational age early on helps guide the timing of tests and monitoring throughout pregnancy, often supported by ultrasound when clinically appropriate.

## 3. Routine Investigations

Blood tests and other investigations are used at various points in pregnancy to monitor your health and your baby's development. Which tests are relevant, and when, depends on your individual pregnancy and history.

## 4. Blood Pressure and Monitoring

Blood pressure and general wellbeing are checked at antenatal visits throughout pregnancy, since changes can be an important early indicator that your doctor needs to be aware of.

## 5. Ultrasound and Scan Planning

Scans are used at different points in pregnancy depending on clinical need — for dating, structural assessment, and monitoring growth. As with early pregnancy, scan timing is individualised rather than fixed to one universal schedule; your doctor will explain what's planned for your pregnancy and why.

## 6. Screening

Depending on your history and preferences, your doctor may discuss various pregnancy screening options with you at appropriate points in your pregnancy, explaining what each screens for and what the results would mean.

## 7. Nutrition and Supplementation

Nutritional needs shift across pregnancy. Supplementation (such as folic acid or iron) should be guided by your doctor based on your individual needs rather than a one-size-fits-all recommendation.

## 8. Vaccination

Vaccination during pregnancy follows current medical recommendations, which your doctor will discuss with you based on your health profile, stage of pregnancy, and local guidance.

## 9. Monitoring Maternal Wellbeing

Beyond physical checks, antenatal visits are also a space to discuss how you're feeling — physically and emotionally — throughout pregnancy. Don't hold back concerns because they seem minor; your care team would rather hear about them.

## 10. Monitoring Fetal Growth and Development

As pregnancy progresses, monitoring shifts increasingly toward tracking your baby's growth and wellbeing, using a combination of clinical assessment and, when indicated, ultrasound.

## 11. Birth Planning

Conversations about your birth plan generally develop through the third trimester, covering your preferences and any relevant medical considerations specific to your pregnancy. There's no need to have all the answers early — this is a collaborative, ongoing conversation.

## 12. Postpartum Planning

Good antenatal care looks beyond delivery. Your doctor will discuss what postpartum monitoring and support will look like for you, so you know what to expect once your baby arrives.

## A Note on Visit Scheduling

Antenatal care has traditionally followed a fixed calendar of visits, but current obstetric guidance is increasingly moving toward **tailored care schedules** — adjusting visit frequency and format (including, in some cases, telemedicine check-ins) based on your individual risk profile rather than applying the same rigid schedule to every pregnancy. What this means for you is simple: your doctor will set out a plan that fits your pregnancy specifically, and it's entirely appropriate to ask why a particular visit or test is (or isn't) being recommended for you.
